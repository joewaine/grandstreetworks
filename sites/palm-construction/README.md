# Palm Construction — client site

A one-page site for Palm Construction WA, Inc. of Federal Way, WA, replacing
their Elementor/WordPress site at palmconstructionwa.com. Built from the
Grand Street Works trade set, but it is a **real client site**, not a
reference build: no `noindex`, no "fictional" footer, and it lives here under
`sites/` rather than `work/` so it can be lifted onto their own domain
unchanged.

The brief: a higher-end design-build remodeler, positioned to win larger
jobs than the site it replaces, keeping the drone reel their current site
opens on. Charcoal, ivory and brass, Cormorant Garamond over Manrope, one
video hero, a large-format work grid, and a consultation form that asks for
a budget range so the enquiries that arrive are the ones they want.

Generated by `tools/build-palm-site.py`. **Do not edit `index.html` by
hand** — change `CONFIG` or the copy tables in the script and rerun:

    python3 tools/build-palm-site.py --page     # rewrite index.html only
    python3 tools/build-palm-site.py            # also re-encode images, identity

## Where the content came from

Everything factual is theirs, lifted from palmconstructionwa.com on
2026-08-31, or from the public register:

- Name, both phone numbers, street address, service-area towns, social
  links: their header, footer, contact page and homepage.
- Services and their descriptions: their nine service pages and cards,
  regrouped into eight (hardscape and landscaping fold into outdoor living;
  interior painting into flooring and interior finishes).
- The numbers — 3,000+ projects in 2025, $250M+ in five years, 50+ years of
  combined leadership experience, "start most projects in less than one
  week", family-owned and veteran-operated, partnerships with Trex, The Home
  Depot and Upgrade, financing "up to 100% of the project": their About and
  homepage. **The 3,000 and $250M figures are company-wide** (they also
  operate in California and Texas); confirm they want them on the Washington
  site.
- "#1 TrexPRO Platinum Partners in Washington": their homepage.
- The four process steps: their "We care about the small details" block
  (permit acquisition and preparation, project planning and design, permit
  expediting and inspection scheduling, final inspection and completion) plus
  the project-manager role described on their careers page.
- Reviews: three of their ten Google reviews (Trustindex widget), verbatim.
- FAQ: ten questions assembled from their FAQ page, About and process copy,
  chosen for what a larger project raises.
- WA L&I **PALMCCW776PS**: from the state open-data register
  (`data.wa.gov`, dataset `m8qx-ubtq`), which lists PALM CONSTRUCTION WA
  INC, construction contractor, general, ACTIVE, effective 2023-10-10,
  expires 2027-10-10, UBI 605321309, at the Federal Way address. Washington
  requires the number in advertising, so it is on the page.
- The mark: their teal-and-gold palm-and-house lockup, redrawn as a
  one-colour monoline roofline with a palm behind it, in brass. `PALETTE` in
  the build script is the one place to change the colours; the original
  logo is in the originals folder if they want it back.

## Video and photographs

**The hero loop is theirs.** Their homepage plays `0430.mp4`, a 40-second
1080p drone montage, as a background video. `tools/encode-hero-video.py`
cut its first ten seconds — the lakefront shot with the pavilion and putting
green — into a 9-second H.264 loop with the tail crossfaded into the head.
Two cuts: `assets/hero/lakefront.mp4` (1920px, CRF 27, 5.3MB) for anything
900px wide or more, and `assets/hero/lakefront-1280.mp4` (1280px, CRF 25,
2.9MB) which the script swaps in on phones, where the hero is cropped to its
middle anyway. The pipeline default is CRF 33 and a 2MB budget, tuned for
Veo plates under a heavy scrim; on real drone footage over water that
encoded visibly blocky (SSIM 0.906 against a near-lossless cut, versus
0.950 at CRF 27), so this site spends the extra bytes. It costs nothing on
first paint: the still frame at t=0.3s is the poster, `preload="metadata"`
means the clip streams in behind the copy, and it fades in once playing.
`prefers-reduced-motion`, print, and connections that report `saveData` or
2G get the still. To re-cut:

    SRC=~/fractal/clients/palm-construction/real/hero-0430.mp4
    python3 tools/encode-hero-video.py $SRC --name lakefront --start 0 --duration 10 \
        --seamless 1 --h264-crf 27 --dest sites/palm-construction/assets/hero
    python3 tools/encode-hero-video.py $SRC --name lakefront-1280 --width 1280 --start 0 \
        --duration 10 --seamless 1 --h264-crf 25 --dest sites/palm-construction/assets/hero
    rm sites/palm-construction/assets/hero/*-poster.jpg    # the build makes its own poster

AV1 and HEVC were measured too (SVT-AV1 CRF 40 matched CRF 27 H.264 at 4.1MB
instead of 5.3MB) and rejected: a 22% saving is not worth software AV1
decode on older laptops for a background loop, and H.264 decodes in
hardware everywhere.

**The gallery is frames from their two reels** (`0430.mp4` and the
Sammamish deck-progress reel) plus four of their own photographs — the
composite deck above Puget Sound (`BG-transformed.jpg`, the only large
photograph on their site), two deck-and-pergola shots at 960px, and the
exterior repaint. The rest of their gallery is 640px WhatsApp photos, too
small for a site at this register; they are in the `real/` folder if
needed. The deck-reel frames carry a Palm watermark bottom-right, so they
are cropped to 4:3 with a left bias to lose it. Captions describe only what
the picture shows; nothing is invented about location or scope.

**Three plates are generated** (Gemini, `tools/gen-palm-images.py`): the
closing band (a covered outdoor room at dusk above the Sound), the financing
band (a white-oak kitchen) and the About photograph (drawings on a table).
They are atmosphere, not portfolio, and they are placed where a photograph
of a specific job would be the wrong register anyway. If Palm has a
finished kitchen or a dusk exterior of their own at 2000px+, swap it in:
drop it in the originals folder under the same name and rerun.

Originals live in `~/fractal/clients/palm-construction/` (`real/` is the
raw pull from their site including both full reels; `originals/` is what
the build encodes). Only the encoded ladder is in the repo.

The deck reel also features their project manager, Benjamin, on camera. His
frames were deliberately not used — ask before putting an employee's face
on the site.

## Before launch

- **Email.** Their site has no email address on it (the contact form posts
  to WordPress), so `CONFIG["email"]` is empty and the consultation form
  falls back to opening a text message to the office number on phones, and
  telling desktop visitors to call. Get the address estimate requests should
  go to, set it, and rerun; or better, point `quote_endpoint` at a real
  endpoint (the intake API in `intake-api/` would do with a second route)
  so the budget and timing fields land in an inbox as data.
- **Confirm with the owners**: the company-wide numbers above, that both
  phone numbers are still answered, the ten-town service area (their
  Sammamish reel suggests they go further; `area_long` says
  "the Seattle–Tacoma area"), and hours, which their site does not state.
- **The Google link** is a search, not a place link, because their site
  carries none. Replace `CONFIG["google"]` with the real Maps URL.
- **Careers and Journal** in the footer point at their current WordPress
  pages. If the new site replaces WordPress entirely, those pages go with it.
- **Their domain.** When they are ready, point palmconstructionwa.com at a
  Render static service publishing this folder (or copy the folder anywhere
  that serves files), and set `CONFIG["url"]` to the real domain so the
  `og:image` and JSON-LD URLs follow. One Google Fonts request (Cormorant
  Garamond, Manrope) is the only external dependency.
