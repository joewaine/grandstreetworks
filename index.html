[SEC_05] What we won't do

Anything compliance-heavy

Payroll, tax filing, accounting of record, clinical records — anything that would put protected health information through a system we built. That stays with the specialists and the software carrying the liability.

Replace software that's good value

Plenty of what you pay for is cheap for what it does, or worth it because everyone else is on it. We'll say so, and it costs you nothing to hear it.

Automate judgement

Some of what looks automatable is the part where your experience shows. Automating it saves an hour and costs a client. That list is in the report too.

Sell you a build the audit didn't justify

If you're already running lean, we'd rather tell you and keep the relationship than invoice you for a project neither of us believed in.

[SEC_08] Fair questions
We're too small for this.

Smaller usually makes the maths better, not worse. In a ten-person firm one person is doing four jobs, so an hour returned to them is an hour returned to four functions. The businesses that get least out of this are the large ones with an operations team already asking the same questions.

We tried ChatGPT and it was underwhelming.

That's the usual experience, and it's the right conclusion from the wrong experiment. Typing questions into a chat box isn't the same as changing how a workflow runs. The audit looks at where the hours actually go — intake, scheduling, quoting, follow-up, double entry — and plenty of the fixes are ordinary software rather than AI.

Is our client data safe?

The first thing the audit produces is a list of what should never leave your systems, and a written rule for your team about what can and can't be pasted into a chat window. Anything we build runs on your accounts and your hosting, and nothing gets write access until you've watched it work with drafts and approvals first. We stay out of clinical records, payroll and anything else carrying regulatory liability.

Who maintains whatever you build?

Your choice. There's an optional monthly care plan covering hosting, patching and small changes, priced well below the subscription it usually replaces — or your own IT can hold it, because the code is documented and it's yours. Declining the care plan doesn't cost you access to anything.

Aren't you just a new kind of lock-in?

You own the repository, the data and the infrastructure accounts, and that's in the contract rather than in a sales conversation. If you want to move to another engineer, everything they need is already in your possession. Replaceable by design.

What if switching breaks something we depend on?

Nothing gets cancelled on a promise. When we replace a tool, old and new run side by side for a full billing cycle, and you only cancel once the new one has done the job for a month with your real work going through it.

What does it cost, and how much of our time does it take?

Everything is a fixed fee agreed before work starts — no hourly billing and no scope-creep conversations. The audit is deliberately the cheapest way in and credits 100% against anything booked in the following 30 days. Your side of it is one 60–90 minute call, a short email thread, and a 30-minute readout.

Tell us what's eating the week.

One email. You'll get a straight answer within a business day on whether an audit is worth your money — including if it isn't.
